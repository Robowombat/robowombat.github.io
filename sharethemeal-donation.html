IMPACT

The background

First of its kind

Back in 2016, when I joined as ShareTheMeal’s only and first in-house designer, there were no other (credible) apps offering a trusted, mobile-first donation experience that felt simple, personal, and impactful. I set out to change that, turning digital giving into a meaningful, accessible habit.
Imagine this: you’re having lunch with a friend and, with one tap, you share 40 cents from your meal with someone in need, out of habit. Giving should feel that easy, and that human.

I designed a donation experience that is transparent and emotionally engaging, unlike the static, transactional feel of traditional charity platforms. While the World Food Programme’s web-based pages showed credibility, they lacked intuitive Interface and relatability that mobile users expect today. The challenge was clear: To design an app that serves a global audience and speaks a universal visual and emotional language, resonating across cultures and generations while maintaining clarity, trust, and impact.
I took the challenge.

The PROduct

From 3 Screens to a Global Donation Experience

ShareTheMeal is a donation-focused mobile app available on Android and iOS. It delivers a seamless end-to-end experience, allowing users to learn about active crisis projects (backed by the United Nations), donate with in-app payments, track impact, and engage in community challenges. When I joined the team, the app was in its very early stages: just a few static screens, a basic donation mechanism, and with around 600,000 users and 8 million shared meals. The intent was strong, and the vision was clear, but the product lacked structure, emotional engagement, and the user experience needed to scale.

understanding users

Designing for a global audience

Our users are literally anyone who is in the position to afford 40 cents, to gift a school meal for someone in need.
The challenge was clear: design for a global audience using a universal language.
Is that even possible? Yes, and no.
‍
Yes, because the motivation behind giving is deeply human. No, because cultural perceptions of money, charity, trust, and urgency vary widely. This meant crafting a user experience that felt intuitive and compelling across countries, generations, belief systems, and tech literacy levels.

I dived into the problem space, I conducted interviews with both existing and not-yet registered potential users. (6 in-depth in-person interviews with not-yet users  (ages 18–45, various countries)

The hypothesis

People are disillusioned with traditional charities.

I believed that today’s users want to contribute quickly and easily, with little patience for middlemen who are seen as “fee‐drainers.” A new, smartphone‐savvy generation demands a modern approach to giving. Although I had an app and a solid concept, I was still far from meeting those expectations.

So, I mapped out user emotions, pain points, consequences, and opportunities across the donation journey of the old design.

problem statement

How might I help users make informed, flexible donations?

How might I build trust and emotional connection for long-term engagement?

The app’s donation experience was functional but limited, and user feedback revealed these key areas of friction:

Key areas of friction:

Lack of choiceUsers wanted to support causes they personally connected with, but the app offered only one global mission. It felt too vague, reducing emotional engagement and perceived relevance.

Low transparencyUsers felt uninformed about where their money was going. Uncertainty around fees, impact, and fund allocation created skepticism and discouraged trust.

Unclear impactThere was little clarity on what each donation achieved. Users struggled to understand the price-to-impact value, which weakened motivation to give.

No follow-upAfter donating, users were left in the dark. There was no continued narrative, update, or emotional connection, just a thank you screen.

These gaps made the act of giving feel disconnected, static, and transactional, when it should have felt intuitive, informed, and meaningful.

I facilitated an internal Design Thinking workshop with 11 participants to brainstorm together, explore solutions around user pain points, expectations, and emotional triggers around the app use.

I delivered concepts in these focus areas:

Donation end-to-end flow:

✔ Designed a more informative mission discovery and campaign selection experience on the Home page, with goal-setting visuals to help users track impact more meaningfully.

✔ Created a new donation screen with clearer, more relatable price-to-impact translations, and a swipeable amount selector featuring playful animations that reflected the growing effect (e.g., one day vs. one month of school meals).

✔ Enhanced trust by highlighting secure payment methods and adding transparency around costs and fees.

✔ Reimagined the post-donation experience, including a more meaningful thank-you screen and personalized follow-up stories from the field.

✔ Improved the subscription feature with a community concept to support recurring donations and encourage habit-building.

Onboarding Experience

✔ Designed an extensive onboarding flow to provide a welcoming, informative start for new users.

✔ Communicated ShareTheMeal’s vision and mission, highlighted its partnership with the UN World Food Programme to build trust.

✔ Enhanced trust by highlighting secure payment methods and adding transparency around costs and fees.

✔ Included options for social sign-in, newsletter opt-in, and subscription promotion to improve retention and engagement early on.

Engagement & Retention

✔ Developed the Profile page to personalize the experience and give users a sense of ownership.

✔ Introduced a basic gamification layer with milestones and achievement badges to celebrate giving habits.

✔ Extended individual donations with a Team feature, allowing users to create or join donation challenges, track team progress, and view leaderboards, enabling companies, schools, or communities to make a joint impact.

✔ Designed Camera Giving feature to boost social engagement: users could take a food photo, apply the custom #ShareTheMeal filtert, donate, and post to social media. The feature launched on World Humanitarian and World Photo Day and was highlighted in Times Square.

Iterative design & user testing

From Insights to Interfaces

I began by exploring multiple concepts for the Home page and donation flow, starting with the information architecture and progressing toward high-fidelity prototypes for user testing. My focus was to ensure emotional engagement, core functionality, and brand perception were all carefully balanced and validated through feedback. Tested ideas like bottom sheets for progressive disclosure, a slider UI for an intuitive and playful donation amount picker, and different placements for custom donation inputs. One of the main challenges was fitting a wide range of content into a single screen without overwhelming the user.

The Tools

As part of my design process I conducted a Design Sprint to explore and ideate together with a team of 11 participants.

  • User journey Mapping based on 2 user types
  • Defining Sprint questions
  • 4 step sketches (Notes, Sketches, Crazy 8, Solution design, Presenting)
  • Low-fi prototyping
  • Building interactive prototypes for testing

Redesigned Home Page

Improvements:

Mission details

Detailed information about each mission to help users make well-informed decisions.

Map view

A map of the country receiving aid, to provide context and geographic awareness.

Swipeable mission cards

Users can choose a mission that resonates with them.

Image galleries

Galleries and video footage build an emotional connection between users and beneficiaries.

Mission goals

A progress bar increases transparency and boosts retention.

Personalized activity

Donation activity from Facebook friends personalizes the experience.

Navigation bar

New content pages increase engagement.

Redesigned Landing experience - Campaign Discovery concept

What DID & didn't work

✅ Clear visual distinction between missions using horizontal-swipe cards, + the new Campaign Goals (progress bar) worked as a strong incentive. 
❌ Removing the iconic large yellow rounded donation button brought an unexpected reaction. Users strongly associated it with the brand. At this early stage, many identified ShareTheMeal as “the app with the big yellow button,” so I kept it.

Redesigned Donation page - Customizable features

Improvements:

Community features

Donate with a team. Create team challenges.

Mission indicator

Shows the currently selected mission country, with the option to change it at this stage.

Trust element

Displays the humanitarian badge (UN WFP) to reinforce credibility and trust.

Visual feedback

Interactive, playful micro-animations reflect the increasing impact of higher donation amounts.

Amount selector

Swipeable selector with clearer, more relatable price-to-impact translations.

Transparency

Added information about costs and fees to increase user confidence.

Payment clarity

Displays the currently selected method and highlights secure payment.

Redesigned Donation experience - iOS

Impact

The launch of the new personalization features (campaign selector, donate as a Team) was followed by a steady user base growth. By the end of 2017 there were 340,450 new users, and 9,610,000 meals shared.

Community & Profile concept - designed & launched

What DID & didn't work

✅  Creating teams and in-app challenges, so that people could collect meals together, proved to be a strong incentive with just a light gamification layer. By December 31, 2017, 9,655 teams had been formed.
‍
❌ In contrast, competitive features, like the public donor rankings and leaderboards, failed to resonate with users, showing that when donations become too much of a competition, people push back.
The public donor rankings performed far worse than initial user feedback suggested.
